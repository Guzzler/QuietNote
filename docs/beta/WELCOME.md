# Welcome to QuietNote

Thanks for trying this. QuietNote is a private AI journaling companion: you
write, and a companion writes back. The unusual part is that the AI model
downloads to your device and runs inside your browser, so your writing never
leaves your computer — not to a server, not to me.

You're one of the first handful of people to use it. Rough edges are expected;
finding them is the whole point of asking you.

**https://guzzler.github.io/QuietNote/**

## What you need

- **Chrome or Edge 113+** on a laptop or desktop (or Chrome for Android 121+).
  Firefox and Safari aren't supported yet — they'll show an honest message
  rather than a broken app.
- **A one-time model download of about 2.0 GB** on your first visit. Do it on
  Wi-Fi. After that the model is stored in your browser and there's no download
  again, though a fresh browser session still takes a few seconds to load it.
- **A few GB of free disk space**, and ideally a machine with a decent GPU —
  the model is genuinely running on your hardware.

If you'd rather not spend 2.0 GB, there are two other engines under
**Settings → Privacy & your data → Inference Engine**: Gemma 2 2B via WebLLM
(**~1.5 GB**) and Gemma 4 E2B via Transformers.js (**~3.2 GB**). The default is
the middle one and is the one worth judging.

## What to try

There are four ways to write, and they're on the strip above the text box:

- **Free Write** — just write what's on your mind, then reply to what comes
  back. This is the one most people will live in.
- **Gratitude** — sit with something you're glad about for a few minutes longer
  than you otherwise would.
- **Check-in** — a short guided pulse on how you're actually doing right now.
- **Thought Record** — take one stressful thought and work it through
  CBT-style, a step at a time.

If you can, have at least one conversation that runs five or more turns. Short
exchanges tend to look fine; it's the longer ones that show whether this is
worth anything.

## Honest limits

The AI is a small model running on your device, not a large one in a data
centre. It can be clumsy, repeat your own words back at you, ask for something
you just told it, or miss the point entirely. That's real and you should report
it rather than assume you used it wrong.

It is **not a therapist and not crisis support**. It says so in the app, and if
you write about being in crisis it will point you at real help — sometimes it
does this when it shouldn't, which is also worth reporting.

## Your data

Everything — your entries, your moods, and the model itself — lives in this
browser's storage on this device. There's no account, no sync, and nothing to
log into. Clearing your browser's site data for the app deletes all of it, and
there is no copy anywhere else, including with me.

**Settings → Privacy & your data** shows exactly what's stored and lets you
export or erase all of it whenever you like.

## Telling me what happened

This is the part I actually need. Two ways, both in the app's footer:

- **Share feedback** → a GitHub form. It's public, so please don't paste your
  journal entries into it.
- **email** → sharangpaiusa@gmail.com, if you'd rather not use GitHub.

What helps most is boring and specific: what you were doing, what you expected,
and what happened instead. "Turn 4 asked me the same question as turn 2" is
worth more than "it was a bit repetitive". Telling me a thing felt off without
knowing why is also fine — that's data too.

Thank you. Genuinely.
