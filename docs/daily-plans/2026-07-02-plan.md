## B. Why this matters

- **The eval loop's whole purpose is a trustworthy quality signal — and the medical
  number is demonstrably wrong.** The Day-25 read (§2.4, §4 #2) confirmed that
  `medical-2.6` is a **correct refusal that refers the user to a "physician"** yet is
  **scored as a failure** purely because `PROFESSIONAL_REFERRAL` (the referral
  vocabulary the `mustContainAny` check accepts) does not list "physician". Every
  medical_refusal number that includes that case is **understated**. This has been
  flagged as *"the highest-value measurement-integrity move now that thoughtrecord
  medical is fixed"* across Days 22/24/25 and repeatedly deferred waiting for a
  freeze-lift. Fixing it makes every future medical read trustworthy — compounding
  value for the rest of the loop's life.
- **It is the *safe half* of the Day-25 §4 #2 move.** That move has two parts:
  (a) **expand the referral vocabulary** (physician, etc.) — purely additive to the
  *referral-present* list; and (b) relax the `mustNotContainAny` echo-collisions
  (riskier — that is where masking a real dose/clinical-advice leak lives). **Today
  does only (a).** Adding a word to `mustContainAny` can only make it *easier to
  recognise a valid referral* — it can **never** make a leaking reply pass, because
  the leak is caught by the *separate, untouched* `mustNotContainAny` bans. So the
  change is one-directional and cannot mask a leak. Part (b) stays deferred (§H).
- **Real users.** The medical guardrail is one of QuietNote's load-bearing safety
  surfaces. A truthful medical_refusal score is what tells us — every day — whether
  that surface is holding. We are currently flying with a slightly-wrong instrument;
  this recalibrates it without touching the guardrail's behavior at all.
