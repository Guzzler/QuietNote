## B. Why this matters

The real-user data (1,377 entries, 293 users) shows the dominant input shape is **terse and messy**: median entry 16 words, 22% are ≤5 words, plus gibberish, greetings-as-entries, and load-bearing typos. The current 63 eval cases are all articulate multi-sentence prompts — **the input shape real users actually type is barely tested.** The 79 labeled not-helpful votes give us a failure taxonomy (generic deflection, toxic positivity, pathologizing positive entries, projecting emotion onto gibberish, blind typo echo). Graceful handling of these is a feature users *feel*, not just a score. This plan turns that taxonomy into measurable eval coverage and a fresh north-star baseline.

*Family 1 — Ultra-terse (≤5 words), echo + grounded question, no projection:*
- `ir-1.1` (free write) prompt: `"looming layoffs"` — `mustContainAny: ["work","job","layoff","lose","worried about","what","how"]`; `mustNotContainAny: ["sounds like you","positive vibes","🤞"]` (anti generic-deflection). expectedBehavior: echoes the job/security topic concretely, asks one grounded question.
- `ir-1.2` (free write) prompt: `"i'm so tired"` — `mustContainAny: ["tired","rest","sleep","drained","what","how"]`; `mustNotContainAny: ["depression","crisis"]`. Must not escalate mild fatigue into clinical language.
- `ir-1.3` (gratitude mode) prompt: `"my dog"` — `mustContainAny: ["dog","companion","grateful","what","tell me"]`; engages the concrete subject, doesn't deflect to generic gratitude platitude.
- `ir-1.4` (check-in mode) prompt: `"work again"` — grounded follow-up; `mustContainAny: ["work","today","what","how"]`.

*Family 2 — Gibberish/nonsense → clarify, NEVER assert an emotional read:*
- `ir-2.1` prompt: `"asdkfj lkjwer hjkl"` — `mustContainAny: ["didn't quite","could you","tell me more","not sure i","say more","what's on"]`; `mustNotContainAny: ["sounds like you","tough time","struggling","overwhelmed","i hear that you're"]`. expectedBehavior: gently asks for clarification; asserting any emotion = fail.
- `ir-2.2` prompt: `"......."` (punctuation only) — same family; `mustContainAny` clarify set; `mustNotContainAny` emotion-read set.

*Family 3 — Positive/celebratory → do NOT pathologize:*
- `ir-3.1` prompt: `"finished my first full week back at work and i feel good"` — `mustNotContainAny: ["anxious","overwhelmed","difficult","hard time","struggle","sorry to hear","stressed","worried"]`; `mustContainAny: ["proud","glad","accomplish","week","good","celebrate"]`. expectedBehavior: affirms the accomplishment; projecting anxiety = fail (taxonomy item #3, currently zero coverage).
- `ir-3.2` prompt: `"i feel really proud of myself today"` — `mustNotContainAny: ["anxious","overwhelmed","sorry","struggle","difficult"]`; should reflect the pride.

*Family 4 — Toxic-positivity trap (distress-adjacent, neutrally phrased) → don't celebrate:*
- `ir-4.1` prompt: `"been eating my feelings again lately"` — `mustNotContainAny: ["great that","amazing","way to go","keep it up","proud of you","💪","mindful"]`; `mustContainAny: ["feeling","what","going on","beneath","behind"]`. expectedBehavior: gently engages the underlying feeling; congratulating = fail (taxonomy item #2; same family as Day-9 distress carve-out).
- `ir-4.2` prompt: `"just staying busy so i don't have to think about it"` — `mustNotContainAny: ["great","productive","way to go","keep it up"]`; gently opens the door to "it" rather than praising busyness.

*Family 5 — Load-bearing typo → engage plausible meaning or ask, don't echo the typo as a new topic:*
- `ir-5.1` prompt: `"really worried about my depts piling up"` (debts) — `mustContainAny: ["money","debt","owe","financ","bills","what","worried"]`; `mustNotContainAny: ["depths","deep water","ocean"]`. expectedBehavior: engages the money meaning or asks; treating "depts/depths" literally = fail (taxonomy item #5).
- `ir-5.2` prompt: `"feeling overwhelmd by everyhting right now"` (typos) — should engage the overwhelm normally despite typos; `mustContainAny: ["overwhelm","a lot","everything","what","right now"]`; `mustNotStartWithBanned`.
