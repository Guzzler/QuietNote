## B. Why this matters

Check-in is the weakest specificity mode on the scoreboard (11/15 — Day-25/28 carried floor). When a user does a morning or evening check-in, the model opens well and closes with a question, but pads a **generic reflective sentence in the middle** — e.g. *"Perhaps we could reflect on how your day unfolded?"* or *"It is completely understandable that you are carrying such a heavy weight right now."* Two costs to the user:

1. **Length/format:** it pushes the reply to 4 sentences, past the "Maximum 3 sentences" cap the prompt already sets — the reply reads padded, not tight and personal.
2. **Specificity:** the filler sentence spends a "move" on something generic instead of echoing the concrete detail the user actually wrote, so the response feels less *heard*.

Empathy is already at ceiling (43/44) and safety is healthy, so this is the highest-value place a small, careful prompt beat can still move quality without risking the safety-critical surfaces.

## E. Concrete implementation plan

### Priority 1 — anti-padding beat (prompt-only)

**File:** `src/prompts/systemPrompts.ts` — edit **both** `CHECKIN_MORNING_INSTRUCTION` and `CHECKIN_EVENING_INSTRUCTION`.

Insert a new beat immediately **before** the existing `Format:` line in each check-in prompt (keep everything else byte-identical). Proposed wording (tune verbatim as needed, but keep it positive/structural — do NOT enumerate banned verbs; the Day-27 NEGATION-PRIMING lesson proved naming discouraged phrasings primes them):

```
NO-FILLER RULE: Your reply has exactly two moves — first name one concrete detail from what the user just wrote, then ask one open question grounded in that detail. Do NOT add a third, generic sentence between them (no "perhaps we could reflect on…", no restating that their feeling is understandable, no summary of the exercise). If a sentence is not either the concrete opening or the closing question, cut it. Two sentences is often better than three; never exceed three.
```

Design rationale / guardrails baked in:
- **Structural, not a blocklist.** It describes the *shape* (two moves) rather than listing forbidden openers, so it won't negation-prime (Day-25/27 lesson). The two illustrative phrases are the exact filler forms seen in the eval data and are given as "no X" examples of the *category*, not a lexical ban — keep them minimal.
- **Reinforces, doesn't contradict, existing rules.** It sits alongside `FIRST LINE RULE` (concrete opener), `END-OF-RESPONSE RULE` (one closing "?"), and `Format: Maximum 3 sentences`. It must not weaken the single-question rule or the "?"-ending rule.
- **Empathy protection.** "First name one concrete detail" preserves the empathy echo that scores 11/11 today; we are cutting the *middle*, not the opener.
- **Scope:** check-in only this pass (one clean variable, matching the critic's mode-scoped finding). Gratitude/thoughtrecord show the same 4-sentence class in the data — if the checkin result is clean and strong, a **follow-up day** may generalize the beat to those two prompts, but do NOT do all five at once today.

**Do NOT touch:** the MEDICAL RULE, SAFETY CARVEOUT, UNINTELLIGIBLE INPUT RULE, crisis detection, guardrails, the AI disclaimer, or `sessionContext.ts`. Freeze gate (evalRunner/evalScorer/prompts-other-than-this-beat/EVAL_CASES) stays otherwise untouched.
