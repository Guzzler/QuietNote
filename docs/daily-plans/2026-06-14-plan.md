## A. Today's objective

Converge QuietNote's privacy messaging on **one persistent, subtle indicator** (the footer) and **one entry point to the privacy/data controls** (inside Settings) — removing the redundant top-level "Privacy" nav button — so the app stops re-selling its privacy story on every surface. Fundamental Problem 4 ("feels like a tech demo, not a journal"), roadmap item A3. Standing guidance: *privacy is already solid — stop re-selling it; focus on core journaling usefulness.*

## B. Why this matters

QuietNote currently asserts "this is private" in at least three places: a dedicated header **Privacy** nav button (Shield), the loading screen ("Your private journaling companion" + a Lock note), and the footer ("Your journal entries stay on this device"). A journal a person trusts does not keep reminding them it's trustworthy — the repetition reads as a *tech demo proving a point*, which is exactly the feeling Track A exists to kill. Consolidating to a single quiet footer indicator (lock + "Stored on this device"), with the actual data controls living one logical level down inside Settings, makes the app feel calmer and more finished without weakening anything: the export/erase functionality stays 100% reachable. This is the cheapest remaining Track-A win and is fully screenshot-verifiable.
